01 / ProblemXUX // SEC-01

The hardware is world-class. The human interaction often isn't.

Space teams build extraordinary systems, then hand them to people who must operate them in the worst conditions a body can work in.

Operating conditions // What the body is up against
Suit pressure4.3 psiGloved hands lose touch and tire fast
LoadVibrationSmall targets become hard to hit
AirCO2 buildupHypercapnia slows thinking
TimeAlarmsSeconds decide the outcome
Two gaps show up again and again
Physical

Tools built for bare hands, used in pressurised gloves

A pressurised suit glove pushes back against every finger movement and dulls the sense of touch. Small knobs, fine triggers and precise grips cause fatigue and errors.

Digital

Every console looks different, and stress makes it worse

Telemetry and control screens vary by vendor and mission. Under vibration, workload and alarm noise, operators lose seconds finding the control that matters.

03 / MethodXUX // SEC-03

Numbers, not adjectives.

Every project follows the same loop, so each design decision can be traced back to a measurement.

01Observe

Watch the real task: ops room, test stand, glove box or analogue mission.

02Measure

Test it under controlled stress: gloves, vibration, time pressure, workload.

03Specify

Write a design rule with numbers, such as minimum target size at a given load.

04Validate

Check it with users, in research and in client pilots.

05Apply

Build it into your product, and into our shared rule set.

Built on existing standards. Our research builds on NASA-STD-3001 (Vol. 2, Human Factors) and ECSS-E-ST-10-11C (Human Factors Engineering), aiming to add measured rules for gloved input and high-stress screens.
04 / ResearchXUX // SEC-04

Two research tracks, running now.

Developed as a Master's thesis at FH JOANNEUM, Graz, in 2026. Results feed directly into our client work.

Track
Question
Output
T1 · Physical

Gloved tool affordances

Which handle, lock and trigger shapes let a gloved hand work with the whole palm, instead of fine finger movements?

Shape rules for hardware

Geometries tested for task time, errors and fatigue, gloved vs. bare-handed.

T2 · Digital

Telemetry under load

How should target sizes, alarm colours and navigation change when operators are shaken, rushed or tired?

Layout rules for screens

Fitts's-law-based sizing under vibration, isolated alarm colour paths and flat navigation.
